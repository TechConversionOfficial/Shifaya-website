## About the business

The Tech Conversion portfolio identifies **Shifaya** as a natural self-care brand aimed at people looking for everyday skincare and herbal beauty products. The likely purchase questions for this type of business are practical: **What is the product? What does it contain? How should it be used? Is it suitable for the shopper? What does it cost, and how is it delivered?** The website needs to answer those questions before a visitor commits to a purchase.

## The problem

Self-care ecommerce depends on a balance between **visual appeal and trustworthy product information**. Someone browsing handmade soaps may be drawn in by a product image or natural-ingredient message, but they still need enough detail to understand the formula, fragrance, quantity, intended use and any relevant precautions.

When a store relies only on broad phrases such as *natural*, *gentle* or *handmade*, the customer may not know what distinguishes one item from another. A useful online buying experience needs readable product names, clear collection grouping, accurate pricing, sensible supporting imagery, and a purchase or enquiry route that works on small screens.

This is the communication and shopping challenge indicated by the project's published positioning, **not an independently documented audit of Shifaya's previous website**.

## Website design and content approach

The priorities below are grounded in Tech Conversion's **published description of the project** and appropriate ecommerce design considerations. They should **not** be interpreted as a claim that every listed component or integration was independently observed on a live Shifaya domain.

1. **Put the natural self-care proposition first.** Introduce the product family and brand purpose in plain language, avoiding exaggerated promises about health or skin outcomes.
2. **Give the product imagery a central role.** Use accurate photographs of the actual soaps or beauty essentials, consistent framing and legible product labels so the items are easy to recognise.
3. **Organise browsing around real product groups.** Collections should reflect the verified inventory rather than categories invented for a portfolio screenshot.
4. **Explain ingredients and usage clearly.** Product descriptions should identify relevant ingredients, net quantity, intended use, care or storage guidance, and any necessary allergy or patch-test information based on approved packaging.
5. **Keep prices and purchasing steps straightforward.** Display current prices and available variants, and make the intended action—shop, add to cart or enquire—easy to locate.
6. **Plan the experience for mobile.** Keep navigation, product photographs, variants and ordering actions usable on narrow screens, where many customers first discover lifestyle products.
7. **Make shopping terms accessible.** Show real delivery coverage, dispatch expectations, return rules, business contact information and payment options only when verified.
8. **Support honest brand storytelling.** If the client documents handmade production, ingredient sourcing or other manufacturing practices, present those facts with appropriate evidence rather than generic sustainability or safety claims.

## Standards the website should be reviewed against

These are **quality references**, not evidence that Shifaya has completed an audit or acquired any certification.

- **[Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals):** assess loading, interaction responsiveness and visual stability, especially on mobile product pages.
- **[WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/):** review keyboard navigation, colour contrast, image descriptions, product controls and form labels.
- **[Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials):** review crawlable product descriptions, useful category content and clear site navigation.
- **[Schema.org](https://schema.org/):** consider accurate Product, Offer, Organisation and BreadcrumbList structured data where supported by the live catalogue.
- **Cosmetic product information:** verify applicable Indian cosmetic labelling and product regulations, manufacturing or importer information, ingredients, net quantity, batch details and warnings for the actual goods sold.
- **Truthful claims:** confirm any *organic*, *chemical-free*, *dermatologically tested*, *skin-safe* or therapeutic claims before publication; natural ingredients do not automatically establish safety for every customer.
- **Ecommerce and privacy:** review real shipping coverage, payments, customer data handling, refund terms and contact information against the seller's current policies.
